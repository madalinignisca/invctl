An estimate from declared nameplate draw. Nothing in this system measures power: {{.Draw.Declaring}} asset(s) contributed a declared draw, and {{.Draw.UndeclaredDraw}} live power input(s) have the supply path recorded but no number typed into it yet — a real gap somebody can go and close, not a claim that those inputs carry no power. Power factor is assumed 1.0 (volt-amps treated as watts) over {{.HoursPerMonth}} hours a month, at a tariff of {{money .TariffMinorPerKWh}} per kWh entered in whole minor units — a real rate like 0.2847 is entered as 28, a systematic understatement of roughly 1.7% before anything else in this estimate is even considered.

{{if .Draw.UnmodelledSites}}{{.Draw.UnmodelledSites}} live site(s) carry no power model at all and are missing from this figure entirely, not merely undercounted.{{else}}Every live site carries at least some power model.{{end}} There is deliberately no percentage next to any of these numbers: asset kind is an open lookup set that grows over time, so no honest denominator exists to divide by, and a percentage that silently stopped counting a new kind would be worse than none. Two things push the figure above low, in addition to the gaps already named: a chassis wired with genuinely independent power rails is counted once, at its larger side, not summed (accepted as the rarer and smaller error); and any unmodelled site named above is not in the total at all.

Not comparable to an all-in hosting quote. It excludes loss in the UPS and distribution above each declared input, and excludes cooling and other facility overhead — a hosting price bundles all of those in. {{if .PUEDeclared}}The facility figure above adds back the declared PUE, but the estimate is still not comparable to a hosting quote without also adding UPS and distribution loss.{{else}}Add them before comparing — declaring INV_POWER_PUE adds back the facility share.{{end}}
