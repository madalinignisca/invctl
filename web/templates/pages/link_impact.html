This cable joins nothing that is modelled

A cable becomes a connectivity edge when its two ends land on assets that forward — members of a network group — in two different groups.

This one does not, which is ordinary rather than wrong: a server's uplink to its own top-of-rack switch has both ends in the same group, so it cannot change what is reachable between groups. Reachability inside a group is answered by the group's own membership, deliberately, because a cable cannot tell you which way traffic flows — so it is declared rather than guessed (docs/reachability-design.md).

The cable is still real, still traced, and unplugging it still matters to whatever is on the end of it. It simply cannot partition the estate, so there is nothing below.

A partition with nothing behind it produces no findings — usually a sign the far side is not yet modelled rather than that it is safe.
