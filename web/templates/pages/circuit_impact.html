Outage simulation

If {{.Circuit.CID}} is cut

The span is gone and the ports at both ends are fine — this asks what stops being reachable when the two places it joins are no longer joined by it. Nothing is taken out of the estate, so anything still reachable another way is correctly reported as unaffected.

{{if not .Cut.Joins}} {{/* The common case, and it must not be shown as an empty result. A circuit ending at the provider is not modelled on their side, so it joins nothing here -- which is a fact about the model, not a finding that cutting it is harmless. Those two look identical in a Result and need opposite sentences. */}}

This circuit joins nothing that is modelled

A circuit becomes a connectivity edge when both ends land on an interface, and both of those interfaces belong to an asset that forwards — a member of a network group — in two different groups.

This one does not, which is ordinary rather than wrong: most circuits end at the provider, and the provider's side of them is not modelled here. Its cost and its contract end are still tracked, and its terminations are still recorded — it simply cannot change what is reachable, so there is nothing below.

{{else if .Cut.Separates}}

Cutting this separates the estate

It is the only path between {{range $i, $g := .Cut.Groups}}{{if $i}} and {{end}}{{$g}}{{end}}, so cutting it leaves the two sides unable to reach each other.

{{if not .HasNetworkFinding}}

Nothing below is reported as unreachable, and that is not a contradiction: the two sides are separated, and nothing currently recorded on the far side depends on reaching this one. A partition with nothing behind it produces no findings — which is worth knowing about a disaster-recovery site, because it usually means the far side is not yet modelled rather than that it is safe.

{{end}}
{{else}}

Another path survives this

This circuit joins {{range $i, $g := .Cut.Groups}}{{if $i}} and {{end}}{{$g}}{{end}}, and cutting it separates nothing — another path already joins them. That is the answer redundancy is bought for, and it is worth knowing it is real rather than assumed.
